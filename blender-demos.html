Authored Blender reference → Wan 3.0 video edit. Each pair places a simple Blender animation beside its video-guided generation so the intended movement can be inspected. These are candidate concept demonstrations, not results from a learned policy or an RSI experiment.

Play each pair together to compare the motion.

01 / Grasp & contact

Reach. Grasp. Lift.

Bring the hand to the mug, establish contact, and lift it from the table. The action reference makes the order and timing explicit.

0:00 / —

Review focus

Hand–object contact. Check whether the hand reaches the mug before it moves, whether both move together, and whether the mug keeps its shape.

02 / Hinge & clearance

Open a path.

Open the door before moving through the doorway. The hinge, opening angle, and body clearance give the action a visible set of constraints.

0:00 / —

Review focus

Order and clearance. Check whether the door rotates around a fixed hinge, opens before the person passes, and stays separate from the body.

03 / Route & ground contact

Go around. Keep moving.

Approach an obstacle, take a clear route around it, and continue toward the destination. The same simple layout anchors the movement across both views.

0:00 / —

Review focus

Path fidelity. Check whether the person follows the intended route, keeps clear of the obstacle, and maintains plausible foot contact with the ground.

A reference for the
larger RSI question.

Here, the action sequences are authored in Blender. The proposed research would replace those fixed choices with a policy, evaluate the rendered consequences, and use failures to revise the process. Accepted trajectories would then become training data for the video model. These pairs illustrate the rendering step; they do not establish that this learning loop works.

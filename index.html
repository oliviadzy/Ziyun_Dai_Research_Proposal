01 / POLICY-GUIDED GENERATION

Give the world a structure.
Give the policy a choice.

The scene is deliberately simple at first. Geometry, a character, and a route make the intended action visible before the video model adds appearance.

01 / BUILD

Code the environment

A coding LLM constructs a small executable scene with objects, traversable space, and explicit state.

Coding LLM → environment
02 / ACT

Control the character

A policy selects actions. The character moves through the white-box world, producing an observable trajectory.

Policy → actions + rollout
03 / RENDER

Generate the visual world

The video model uses the scene and trajectory as conditioning to render a richer, action-dependent future.

Video model → visual future
04 / VERIFY

Judge what happened

Check whether the render follows the intended action, preserves the scene, and respects physical constraints.

Verifier → feedback + selection

The research target: controllable generation in which changing the policy’s action changes the rendered future in a predictable way.

02 / RECURSIVE SELF-IMPROVEMENT

Every round feeds
the next one.

RSI is the central idea. Failures improve how the system explores and evaluates; good trajectories become training data for the video model.

Branch A / learn from failure

Improve the process.

A collision, a missed turn, or a disappearing object becomes a trace to inspect. The outer loop diagnoses the cause, proposes a targeted change, and tests it on new rollouts.

Failure → diagnose → revise → re-test ↻
What changes: the policy or planner, environment code where needed, and evaluation or data-selection rules. Each proposed change needs an independent check.
Branch B / learn from good experience

Improve the video model.

Retain trajectories that pass the checks, together with their states, actions, and rendered frames. Use this selected experience to fine-tune the video model for the next round.

Verified rollout → training set → video update ↻
What changes: the video model’s weights, starting with fine-tuning on selected trajectories. Reward-guided generative RL would be evaluated as a further training strategy.
Research question

Can recursive exploration and verified self-generated data make a video world model more controllable and physically consistent?
A hypothesis to test through future training and held-out evaluation.

01 / Grasp & contact · Concept demo

Reach. Grasp. Lift.

A hand approaches the handle, makes contact, and lifts the mug. The reference makes the action order visible before the scene is rendered.

0:00 / —

Policy choice / proposed
Choose the approach, hand alignment, and moment to lift.
Failure → RSI / proposed
If the hand misses or the mug slips, revise the grasp timing and test another rollout.
Accepted → training / proposed
Only a checked, stable hand–mug trajectory would enter the video-model training set.
02 / Hinge & clearance · Concept demo

Open a path.

Open the door, make room, then step through. Hinge motion and body clearance give the proposed policy concrete constraints to act on.

0:00 / —

Policy choice / proposed
Choose how far to open the door and when to move through.
Failure → RSI / proposed
If the body clips the door or moves too early, revise the opening angle and action sequence.
Accepted → training / proposed
A trajectory with checked contact, clearance, and continuity would become training data.
03 / Route & ground contact · Concept demo

Go around. Keep moving.

Move around a planter and continue toward a destination. A shared layout lets the intended route be compared with the generated motion.

0:00 / —

Policy choice / proposed
Choose a route and pace that keep the character clear of the obstacle.
Failure → RSI / proposed
If the path cuts through the planter or feet slide, revise the route and movement timing.
Accepted → training / proposed
A checked route with consistent scene geometry and ground contact would be retained.

The contribution to pursue is the RSI loop: use failures to revise the process, test those revisions on new rollouts, and use accepted experience to fine-tune the video model for the next round.
